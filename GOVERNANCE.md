## Decision-making

- **Day-to-day decisions** (bug fixes, small features, documentation, dependency patch/minor bumps): any maintainer can act alone. With a single maintainer, that's simply how it works today.
- **Significant decisions** (new dependencies, protocol/API changes, major version bumps, changes to this governance document): once the project has more than one maintainer, these require agreement from **at least 2 maintainers**. With a single maintainer, this rule is dormant but stays written down for when the project grows.
- Decisions are made by consensus first; if consensus can't be reached among maintainers, a simple majority vote decides.

## Becoming a maintainer

New maintainers are added by **unanimous agreement** of the existing maintainers, when the project's workload or scope genuinely needs it — not on a fixed schedule. Interested contributors should have a track record of quality, sustained contributions and good judgment shown through issues and pull requests over time.

## Maintainer responsibilities

- Triage new issues and pull requests within the response times set in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) and [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), on a best-effort basis.
- Uphold the [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and act on reports made under it.
- Keep [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md) and [`docs/ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS.md`](docs/ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS.md) up to date with significant changes.
- Communicate openly: decisions and their reasoning should be visible in issues/PRs, not made privately, so anyone following the project can understand why things are the way they are.

## Conflict resolution

Disagreements are first worked out through open discussion in the relevant issue or pull request. If that doesn't resolve it, it goes to a maintainer vote as described above. Conduct-related conflicts follow the reporting and enforcement process in [`CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) instead.
